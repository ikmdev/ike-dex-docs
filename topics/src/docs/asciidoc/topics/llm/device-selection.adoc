= LLM-assistance in Selecting IVD Devices for Scaling

indexterm:[automation, DeX record scaling]
indexterm:[device data, product code]
indexterm:[device data, IVD device]
The team experimented with reducing manual effort in selecting
IVD devices by using an LLM to curate a set of 150{plus} IVD
devices to support throughput of DeX record creation.
First, the LLM categorized devices by FDA product code and grouped
them into seven categories:
at-home tests,
chemistries,
genomics, infectious diseases,
sexually transmitted infections (STI) and human
immunodeficiency virus (HIV),
vaccine-preventable diseases,
and immunology and hematology.
These categories were selected and verified by the team to
cover a range of test types and to identify which areas were
best to scale further.
For example,
chemistries and genomics were the largest categories,
and they may be a focus area to scale additional IVD devices.
Second, the LLM filtered through millions of devices on GUDID,
identifying approximately 8,000 IVD devices with a linked 510(k) number.
Third, the team used the LLM to set quotas for each IVD category and
assign product codes to a tier based on the availability of
decision summaries and package inserts.
These tiers were:

* Pilot: Product codes from which the team has already collected devices and created DeX records through our Version 1 (v1) model.
* Tier A: Product codes with devices that have a linked decision summary and package insert, and that describe the test kit itself.
* Tier B: Product codes with devices that have a decision summary and package insert, but that consist of associate devices such as reagents, controls, and calibrators.
* Tier C: Product codes with devices that lack a decision summary or package insert.

indexterm:[automation, tiering]
This tiering system allowed the team to focus their efforts on
the product codes most likely to contribute to the scaling goal,
prioritizing IVD devices with the most available data while
maintaining coverage across categories.
By using an LLM to manage categorization,
filtering,
and tiering,
the team has significantly reduced the manual effort required
to collect these devices and established a more efficient,
organized foundation for scaling DeX record production of
future IVD devices.
