= Clinical Evidence Council (CEC) Presentation Preparation and Overview

== Content Overview

indexterm:[stakeholder engagement, OHT7 working session]
indexterm:[data quality, real-world data]
The presentation described is the planned working-session
material for OHT7 reviewers,
presented by Dr. Keith Campbell.
It has three objectives:
explain how DeX simplifies review,
demonstrate how it supports decision-making through a 510(k)
substantial equivalence example,
and gather OHT7 input to identify and prioritize reviewer pain points.
It follows the earlier DeX presentations to CEC (from May and
August 2026) but shifts from a general overview of RWD quality
to a reviewer-centered conversation about the premarket review workflow.

indexterm:[device data, GUDID]
indexterm:[terminology, LOINC]
indexterm:[terminology, SNOMED CT]
The deck first frames the problem.
A CDRH reviewer currently cross-checks four or more
disconnected sources to answer routine questions,
such as which versions of a predicate are on the market,
which terminology standard code identifies a test,
whether the sponsor chose the right product code,
and whether labeling claims match the submission data.
The sources are GUDID,
clinical terminology databases such as Logical Observation
Identifiers Names and Codes (LOINC^©^) and Systematized
Nomenclature of Medicine,
Clinical Terms (SNOMED CT^©^),
510(k) product classifications,
package inserts and Indications For Use (IFUs),
and 510(k) decision summaries.
DeX is introduced as a structured,
public-sources-only reference database that “extends” GUDID with linked,
cited attributes for each device,
with one record per device.
DeX is not an instance database,
a store of confidential submissions,
or a holder of patient or test-level data.
Additionally,
an example of a BioFire Respiratory Panel record shows a full
set of attributes in a single view,
and a side-by-side comparison shows how fields line up directly
between devices.

indexterm:[regulatory science, substantial equivalence]
indexterm:[device data, GMDN]
The core of the session is a walkthrough of the 510(k)
substantial equivalence decision flowchart,
which maps where DeX removes manual steps.
These include comparing indications for use against a predicate,
extracting predicate information (e.g.
K number and product code),
and building comparison tables for GMDN codes,
analytes, detection technologies,
specimen types,
and instrument platforms.
The deck’s with/without comparison uses GeneXpert XPRS4PLEX-10
as the worked example.
Without DeX,
data and codes must be pulled from individual summaries,
package inserts,
and separate databases,
with no single verifiable source of truth for comparison.
With DeX, reviewers get a side-by-side comparison with values traceable
back to specific records.
The deck also positions substantial equivalence support as
“today’s focus” among broader use cases:
post-market RWD quality improvement,
device submission integrity (checking submitted LOINC^©^
mappings against what an assay actually detects),
LLM grounding,
cross-device comparability at scale,
and cross-cutting domain exploration beyond IVDs.

indexterm:[device data, contact lens]
indexterm:[device data, UDI exception]
The deck then moves from IVDs to a non-IVD example utilizing
contact lenses data which shows that DeX is a general-purpose
way to represent a device rather than an IVD-only form.
Contact lens data is not yet required in GUDID.
FDA granted most soft contact lenses an exception from UDI data
submission because their thousands of parameter variations
exceed what GUDID can currently handle,
and the exception lasts until one year after FDA delivers a
technical solution.
As a result,
GUDID records today are voluntary,
uneven, and at times inaccurate.
DeX adds consistent encodings and relationships across material codes,
GMDN, and GUDID,
plus family grouping by UDI.
A proposed next step for discussion with the audience is to
build a contact lens DeX record from a CDRH “golden-set” lens,
review it with CDRH,
and collaborate with SMEs on attributes,
GMDN codes,
and SPL examples.

indexterm:[knowledge exchange, DeX record]
indexterm:[automation, AI-assisted conversion]
The deck closes with the status,
next steps,
and the discussion which will become the main
stakeholder-engagement output.
Today there are 20+ DeX records,
which can be used to compare device predicates and extract
device characteristics across sources.
Next steps are scaling curation through LLM-assisted,
SME-validated workflows and expanding from IVDs to other categories,
with the longer-term vision that every medical device has a DeX record.
